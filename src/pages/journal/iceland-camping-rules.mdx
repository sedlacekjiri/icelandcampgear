Iceland's camping regulations changed significantly in 2015, when the country introduced rules restricting wild camping in response to increased tourist numbers and damage to fragile landscapes. Understanding the rules before you arrive will help you enjoy Iceland responsibly — and avoid fines.

## The Basic Rule: No Wild Camping in Settled Areas

Since 2015, camping is **not permitted outside of designated campsites** within 200 metres of a public road in areas with a farmhouse or other building visible. This covers most of Iceland's accessible countryside.

In practice, this means:
- You cannot camp in a field by the road, even if it looks perfect
- You cannot camp in a car park or pull-off area
- Sleeping in a campervan/car in these areas may also be restricted in some municipalities

**Penalties**: Fines for illegal camping have increased significantly. Reykjavík municipality has issued fines of up to **60,000 ISK** for camping in parks and non-designated areas.

## Where You CAN Camp

### 1. Designated campsites
Iceland has 170+ official campsites across the country. These range from simple field sites to well-equipped facilities with showers, cooking areas, and electricity. Always the safest and most sustainable option.

### 2. With landowner permission
You may camp on private land if you have **explicit permission from the landowner**. Ask first — most Icelanders are friendly and may say yes, especially for a single night.

### 3. Uncultivated wilderness areas
In remote wilderness areas (away from roads, farms, and settlements), wild camping is generally permitted under the principle of [Outdoor Access Rights](https://www.ust.is/the-environment-agency-of-iceland/). However:
- Stay no more than **3 nights** in the same spot
- Leave absolutely no trace
- Respect any posted restrictions
- This applies mainly to the highlands and remote fjords — not the populated parts of Iceland

### 4. National Parks
National parks have their own rules that vary by park:
- **Vatnajökull**: Camping only at designated sites (Skaftafell, Svínafell, etc.)
- **Snæfellsjökull**: Designated sites only within the park
- **Þingvellir**: Camping only at Leirar campsite

## Fire Regulations

**Open fires are effectively prohibited in Iceland.**

This is one of Iceland's strictest camping rules and it's for good reason — Iceland's sparse vegetation is extremely flammable and slow to recover. The rules:

- No campfires in the open countryside
- Never light a fire on, near, or in sensitive vegetation areas
- Camp stoves are fine and are the only permitted cooking method
- Even in designated fire pits (rare), check current conditions — fire bans are common in dry summers

Violations can result in significant fines and, if damage occurs, civil liability.

## The Camping Card

The [Camping Card (Tjaldferðakort)](https://www.campingcard.is) is a pre-paid pass that covers 1 adult at 35+ designated campsites for **28 nights**. Cost: approximately **21,900 ISK**.

If you plan to use campsites regularly, this is excellent value. Additional adults can be added. Children under 12 are typically free.

## Campsite Fees Without a Camping Card

Expect to pay **1,500–2,500 ISK per adult per night** at most campsites. Children's rates vary. Payment is usually by card — carry a card, not just cash.

## Campsite Etiquette

While not legally binding, these norms are widely respected:

- **Quiet hours**: 23:00–07:00 at most sites
- **No loud music** — Iceland's campsite culture is peaceful
- **Dogs**: Allowed at most sites but must be on a leash
- **Waste**: Use bins provided or pack out what you pack in
- **Showers**: May require a coin or token — bring small change or a card

## Leave No Trace

Iceland's landscapes look robust but are fragile. The surface of Iceland is often thin volcanic soil over ancient moss — a single footstep off a path can damage growth that takes centuries to recover.

Key Leave No Trace principles for Iceland:

1. **Stay on marked paths** — off-path walking in sensitive areas is damaging and increasingly regulated
2. **Take all litter** with you — there are no bins in remote areas
3. **Never disturb birds or wildlife** — ground-nesting birds are common and easily stressed
4. **Don't pick plants or moss** — it's illegal in protected areas
5. **Use campsite toilet facilities** — never near water sources or vegetated areas
6. **Pack out human waste** in wilderness areas where no facilities exist

## Summary: Know Before You Go

| Situation | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| Near a road with buildings | Campsite only |
| Private land | Permission required |
| Remote wilderness | Generally permitted, max 3 nights |
| National park | Designated sites only |
| Open fire | Prohibited |
| Campsite without card | ~2,000 ISK/person/night |

Camping in Iceland is still one of the world's great experiences — the rules are designed to protect that experience for future generations. When in doubt, use a designated campsite and leave no trace. Iceland's nature has welcomed you; leave it better than you found it.
