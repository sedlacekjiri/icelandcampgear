Iceland is one of the most rewarding places in the world to camp — but only if you come prepared. The weather is famously changeable, temperatures are lower than most visitors expect, and the wind can be genuinely extreme. The good news: with the right gear, you can stay comfortable in almost any conditions.

Here's our complete packing list, built from years of camping across Iceland.

## What to Rent vs. What to Bring

Before we get into the list: most camping hardware is much better rented than brought from home. Tents, sleeping bags, stoves, and cooksets are bulky, heavy, and expensive to check on flights. Renting from us saves you luggage fees and guarantees Iceland-appropriate gear.

**Rent from us**: tent, sleeping bag, sleep pad, stove, cookset, lantern, headlamp, camp chairs, cooler, trekking poles, rain gear, base layers, dry bags.

**Bring from home**: clothes for daily wear, toiletries, medications, electronics, travel documents.

## Shelter

If you're renting:
- ✓ 4-season tent (we handle this)
- ✓ Sleeping bag (−6°C or −10°C depending on season)
- ✓ Sleeping pad (R-value 4+)

If you own gear: make sure your tent has taped seams, a full bathtub groundsheet, and good wind resistance. Freestanding tents should always be pegged even if they seem stable — Iceland winds are deceptive.

## Clothing

This is where most visitors underestimate Iceland. The layering system is everything.

**Base layer** (2× recommended):
- Merino wool top and bottom. Merino regulates temperature, wicks moisture, and resists odour. Worth every penny.
- Avoid 100% cotton — it retains moisture and can cause hypothermia in wet/cold conditions.

**Mid layer** (1–2×):
- Fleece jacket — for warmth around camp or as an insulating layer under a hardshell.
- Down or synthetic insulated jacket — for evenings, the highlands, or any shoulder-season trip.

**Outer layer (waterproofs)**:
- Hardshell jacket — fully waterproof and windproof. This is your most important item. It will rain.
- Hardshell trousers — equally important. A good pair of waterproof trousers can be the difference between a great day and a miserable one.

**Everyday wear**:
- Quick-dry hiking trousers (2 pairs)
- T-shirts (2–3) — merino if possible
- Fleece or hoodie for camp evenings
- Lightweight down jacket for cold nights

**Footwear**:
- Waterproof hiking boots — essential. Trail runners are fine in dry conditions but Iceland is rarely dry.
- Camp sandals or lightweight shoes for around the campsite
- Warm socks (3–4 pairs, wool preferred)

**Accessories**:
- Warm beanie — the top of your head loses a lot of heat
- Lightweight gloves (even in summer — nights get cold)
- Neck gaiter or buff
- Sunglasses (Iceland's light can be intense)
- Sun hat (yes, you'll want one in summer)

## Cooking & Kitchen

If renting:
- ✓ Stove and fuel
- ✓ Cookset
- ✓ Utensils

Bring from home:
- Small cutting board and knife (a good folding knife is ideal)
- Spices and condiments in small containers — camp food is much better seasoned
- Collapsible water bottles (2 per person)
- Water purification tablets as backup
- Tea, coffee, hot chocolate — hot drinks at camp are vital morale boosters
- A few days of emergency food (energy bars, instant noodles)

---

## Electronics & Navigation

- **Phone** with offline maps downloaded (Gaia GPS, Maps.me, or Google Maps offline)
- **Power bank** — large capacity (20,000mAh+). You'll drain your phone using GPS and camera. Cold weather reduces battery life significantly.
- **Charging cables** — bring extras
- **Camera** — Iceland is extraordinarily photogenic
- **Satellite communicator** — we strongly recommend a Garmin inReach Mini 2 for anyone going to the highlands or remote areas. We rent these — ask when booking.

---

## Safety & Health

- **First aid kit** — basic at minimum: plasters, antiseptic wipes, blister treatment, ibuprofen, antihistamines
- **Emergency whistle** — small, light, and could save your life
- **Emergency bivvy/space blanket** — takes up almost no space and keeps you alive in a survival situation
- **Sunscreen SPF 50** — Icelandic sun is deceptively strong, especially near glaciers
- **Insect repellent** — midges can be a nuisance around Lake Mývatn in summer

## Documents & Money

- Passport or ID (required for car rental)
- Driver's licence
- Travel insurance details (make sure it covers outdoor activities)
- Emergency contacts written down (not just saved in your phone)
- Credit card + some cash (most places accept cards, but not all)

---

## Campsite Essentials

- Camping Card (Tjaldferðakort) if you're visiting multiple campsites — excellent value
- Eye mask — essential during midnight sun season (June/July)
- Earplugs — campsite noise and wind
- Microfibre towel (2 ideally — one for showering, one for dishes)
- Biodegradable soap
- Small trowel (for dig-and-bury waste if wild camping)
- Duct tape — fixes almost anything
- Zip-lock bags — for wet gear, electronics protection, food storage

---

## What NOT to Bring

- **Cotton jeans** — heavy, slow to dry, and potentially dangerous in cold/wet conditions
- **Regular umbrella** — useless in Iceland's wind
- **Too many shoes** — you'll thank yourself for packing light
- **Bulky camping hardware** — rent instead of checking a second bag

---

## Quick Seasonal Adjustments

**Summer (Jun–Aug)**: Focus on layers and rain gear. Eye mask is essential. Midges possible around Mývatn.

**Spring/Autumn (May, Sep–Oct)**: Upgrade your sleeping bag. Add extra warm layers. Expect shorter days and more dramatic weather.

**Winter (Nov–Apr)**: Not for beginners. Four-season tents only, −20°C+ sleeping bags, crampons for icy terrain.
