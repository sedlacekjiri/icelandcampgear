Iceland has over 170 official campsites — from basic field pitches to well-equipped sites with hot showers, cooking facilities, and stunning views. Choosing where to stay is one of the most enjoyable parts of planning an Iceland camping trip.

Here are our favourites, organised by region.

## Reykjavík & South West

### Reykjavík Campsite (Laugardalur)
The largest campsite in Iceland and the natural starting point for most trips. Located in Laugardalur park, 3 km from the city centre. Facilities are excellent — hot showers, laundry, cooking area — and you can walk or cycle into town. Busy in peak season but well-managed.

**Best for**: First/last nights, gear collection, solo travellers new to camping.

### Þórsmörk — Húsadalur
Accessible only by 4WD or foot, Þórsmörk (Thor's Forest) is one of Iceland's most magical landscapes. Three separate campsites in the valley: Húsadalur, Langidalur, and Básar. Surrounded by glaciers and birch forests. The end/start point of the famous Laugavegur Trek.

**Best for**: Trekkers, nature lovers, anyone who wants to escape the crowds.

## South Coast

### Skaftafell (Vatnajökull National Park)
One of Iceland's premier campsites, set against the dramatic backdrop of Vatnajökull glacier and black basalt cliffs. Excellent facilities and brilliant hiking trails nearby, including the walk to Svartifoss waterfall. Can be busy — arrive early in peak season.

**Best for**: Families, glacier walking, multi-day base camp.

### Jökulsárlón Campsite
Right next to the famous glacier lagoon, this simple campsite offers one of the most dramatic settings you'll find anywhere. Wake up to icebergs drifting past. Sunsets and sunrises over the lagoon are extraordinary.

**Best for**: Photography, once-in-a-lifetime views, couples.

### Vik Campsite
A small, well-run campsite in the charming village of Vík. Walking distance from Reynisfjara black sand beach. The view from the hill above town (where the church sits) is spectacular.

**Best for**: First-time campers, easy access to South Coast highlights.

## East Iceland

### Hallormsstaðarskógur Forest Campsite
Iceland's largest forest surrounds this peaceful campsite near the shores of Lagarfljót lake. Birch trees provide rare shelter from the wind. Quiet, uncrowded, and genuinely beautiful in autumn.

**Best for**: Rest days, families, autumn travellers.

### Seyðisfjörður
This picturesque fjord village — reached by a spectacular mountain road — has a campsite right by the waterfront. The village itself is Iceland's most artsy, with colourful houses and regular cultural events.

**Best for**: Culture lovers, ferry travellers (the Smyril Line ferry docks here), cyclists.

## North Iceland

### Hveravellir (Highlands)
The crown jewel of Icelandic campsites. Located on the Kjölur highland route, Hveravellir is an oasis of geothermal hot springs in the middle of the desolate interior plateau. Soak in the natural hot pot under the stars — or the midnight sun.

**Best for**: Highland explorers, anyone wanting the ultimate Iceland experience.
**Note**: Accessible only by 4WD, late June to September.

### Lake Mývatn — Hlíð Campsite
Set on the shores of this volcanic lake, the Hlíð campsite is one of the most popular in the north — and deservedly so. Mývatn is one of Iceland's most geologically dramatic areas. Explore pseudo-craters, lava formations, mud pools, and bathing spots.

**Best for**: Geology lovers, birdwatchers, those who want to stay multiple nights.

### Ásbyrgi Canyon Campsite
Located inside Ásbyrgi, a horseshoe-shaped canyon that feels like a secret world. Birch woodland fills the canyon floor. Peaceful and sheltered. One of Iceland's most atmospheric campsites.

**Best for**: Quiet explorers, families, hikers.

## West Iceland

### Grundarfjörður
A small, well-kept campsite at the base of the iconic Kirkjufell mountain — arguably the most photographed mountain in Iceland. Worth getting up early (or staying up late) for the light.

**Best for**: Photography, Snæfellsnes Peninsula explorers.

### Arnarstapi Campsite (Snæfellsnes)
Located on the dramatic basalt coastline near the Snæfellsjökull glacier. Otherworldly landscape of sea stacks, lava arches, and seabird colonies.

**Best for**: Nature lovers, those completing the Snæfellsnes Peninsula loop.

---

## Landmannalaugar (Highlands)

Deserves its own special mention. Landmannalaugar is a highland destination that sits at the start of the Laugavegur Trek and offers the most colourful landscape in Iceland — rainbow-striped rhyolite mountains, steaming hot springs, and wildflower meadows.

The campsite here is basic (toilets, no showers — there's a natural hot spring for bathing) but the setting is extraordinary. One of the most memorable nights of camping you'll ever have.

**Access**: 4WD only, via F225 or F208. Open late June to early September.

## Tips for Campsite Stays

1. **Book in advance** for peak season (June–August) at popular sites like Skaftafell, Þórsmörk, and Landmannalaugar.
2. **Arrive early in the day** at popular sites — prime spots fill up fast.
3. **Get the Camping Card** if you're visiting 6+ campsites — it pays for itself quickly.
4. **Be quiet between 23:00 and 07:00** — respected across all Icelandic campsites.
5. **Pick up after yourself and your dog** — Iceland's campsite culture relies on mutual respect.
