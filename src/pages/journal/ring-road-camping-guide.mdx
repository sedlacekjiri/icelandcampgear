Iceland's Ring Road (Route 1) is one of the world's great road trips. This 1,322-kilometre loop encircles the entire island, passing glaciers, waterfalls, volcanic plains, black sand beaches, and some of the most dramatic scenery on Earth. Doing it by campervan or car with a tent is the most immersive — and affordable — way to experience it.

Here's everything you need to know.

## When to Go

The Ring Road is technically driveable year-round, but the best camping conditions are:

- **June to August**: Peak season. Long daylight hours (or midnight sun in June/July), most campsites open, temperatures 10–15°C in the lowlands. Expect crowds at major attractions.
- **May and September**: Shoulder season. Fewer crowds, dramatic autumn colours in September, some campsites may be closed or have limited facilities.
- **October to April**: Not recommended for camping unless you're very experienced. Many campsites close, roads can be icy or blocked, and temperatures are severe.

## What to Expect Along the Route

### South Coast
Starting from Reykjavík and heading east along the south coast, you'll encounter:

- **Seljalandsfoss & Skógafoss**: Two of Iceland's most iconic waterfalls, both easily accessible from the Ring Road.
- **Reynisfjara Black Sand Beach**: Stunning but dangerous — waves are unpredictable. Never turn your back on the ocean here.
- **Skaftafell (Vatnajökull National Park)**: Excellent campsite, great base for glacier walks and Svartifoss waterfall.
- **Jökulsárlón Glacier Lagoon**: Unmissable. Icebergs floating in a lagoon connected to the sea. Camp at the nearby campsite and catch the golden hour light.

### East Fjords
Often overlooked by visitors who rush the route, the East Fjords reward those who slow down:

- Dramatic fjord scenery, quiet roads, and excellent birdwatching.
- Seyðisfjörður village is one of Iceland's most charming — worth a night's detour.
- Wild reindeer are commonly spotted in this region.

### North Iceland
- **Lake Mývatn**: Volcanic landscape unlike anywhere else. The campsite here is excellent. Allow 2 nights.
- **Dettifoss**: Europe's most powerful waterfall. Remote but accessible from the Ring Road via a short detour.
- **Húsavík**: The whale watching capital of Europe. The campsite has direct harbour views.
- **Akureyri**: Iceland's second city. Good for resupplying food and fuel.

### West Iceland & Snæfellsnes Peninsula
- **Snæfellsnes Peninsula**: This is worth the detour west from Borgarnes. The glacier-capped Snæfellsjökull volcano is legendary (Jules Verne set his Journey to the Centre of the Earth here).
- **Grundarfjörður**: One of Iceland's most photographed mountains, Kirkjufell, is here — and there's a great campsite nearby.

## Essential Gear for the Ring Road

The Ring Road takes you through all of Iceland's climate zones. You need gear that handles everything:

**Shelter**: A quality 4-season tent is non-negotiable. Iceland's coastal winds can be ferocious — a cheap festival tent will not survive. We recommend the MSR Hubba Hubba or Hilleberg Nallo for couples, or the Outwell Cloud 4 for families.

**Sleeping**: Temperatures regularly drop to 5°C at night even in summer. A sleeping bag rated to at least −6°C is recommended. Pair it with a good sleeping pad (R-value 4+) to insulate from the cold ground.

**Cooking**: You'll need a reliable stove — we recommend the MSR PocketRocket 2 for backpackers or the Campingaz 2-burner for car campers. Gas canisters are widely available at N1 petrol stations.

**Rain gear**: It will rain. Iceland averages 200+ rainy days per year. A quality waterproof jacket and trousers are essential, not optional.

**Water**: Most campsite taps have excellent drinking water. For free camping in remote areas, a water filter (like the Katadyn BeFree) lets you drink directly from streams.

## Campsite Costs

Campsite fees on the Ring Road are typically **1,500–2,500 ISK per person per night**. The Camping Card (Tjaldferðakort) costs around **21,900 ISK** and covers 1 adult at 35 designated campsites for 28 nights — excellent value if you're doing a full loop.

## Safety Tips

1. **Always check road conditions** at vegagerdin.is before driving. F-roads (highland roads) require 4WD and are often closed.
2. **Register your itinerary** with the Icelandic Association for Search and Rescue (SAR) at safetravel.is. It's free and could save your life.
3. **Never camp outside designated campsites** on private land — it's illegal. In national parks and wilderness areas, wild camping is permitted but strictly regulated.
4. **Fuel up whenever you can** — petrol stations can be 100+ km apart in remote areas.
5. **Weather changes fast** — what looks like a clear morning can turn into a storm within an hour. Always have layers accessible.

## The Ring Road in Numbers

- **Total distance**: 1,322 km
- **Recommended time**: 7–14 days (2 weeks lets you breathe)
- **Campsites along the route**: 40+
- **Average campsite cost**: 2,000 ISK/person/night
- **Best months**: June–August
